# Atoms for Humanity — Visual Brand & Website Design Specification

## 1. Core Brand Idea

Atoms for Humanity should look like a **scientific institution built by exceptional software engineers**.

The visual identity should communicate, before the visitor reads much copy:

> **This is a technically serious, future-oriented organization building foundational scientific capabilities for a consequential human purpose.**

The website should combine:

* the precision and polish of Stripe, Linear, and Vercel
* the intellectual seriousness of a modern research institute
* a subtle environmental signal
* the restraint and credibility of a nonprofit scientific institution

It should **not** look like:

* a university research-group website
* an academic open-source project
* an environmental advocacy organization
* a biotech startup
* a generic AI startup
* a Web3 / crypto site
* a corporate sustainability consultancy

The intended reaction is:

> “These people understand science, software, and systems at a very high level.”

followed by:

> “And they are applying that capability to an unusually important mission.”

---

# 2. Brand Personality

The visual system should feel:

**Technically sophisticated**
The dominant impression.

**Precise**
Everything should appear deliberately placed and engineered.

**Future-oriented**
But without science-fiction visual tropes.

**Scientific**
Diagrams, molecular language, data, and computational concepts should feel authentic rather than decorative.

**Calm**
Avoid visual noise, excessive animation, gradients everywhere, or aggressive calls to action.

**Mission-driven**
The environmental and human purpose should remain visible beneath the technical sophistication.

**Open and trustworthy**
The organization should feel transparent rather than mysterious or exclusive.

---

# 3. Design Principle

The central design principle is:

> **Technical darkness punctuated by signs of life.**

The primary environment should be dark graphite with subtle green undertones.

Against that:

* off-white typography provides clarity
* technical teal-green provides energy
* occasional pale surfaces provide breathing room
* scientific graphics provide texture
* whitespace provides sophistication

The green should feel like **technology becoming biological**, not “eco branding.”

Avoid leaves, forests, recycling iconography, bright lime green, or environmental stock imagery.

---

# 4. Overall Visual Direction

## Primary appearance

Dark-first.

However, **do not use pure black**.

The site should use very dark graphite / charcoal surfaces with slight green or blue-green undertones.

Think:

**graphite + glass + scientific instrumentation + restrained biological signal**

rather than:

**black + neon + cyberpunk**

Sections can alternate subtly between:

* deep graphite
* slightly elevated graphite-green
* occasional warm off-white / pale mineral sections

A light section should appear periodically to prevent the site from becoming visually oppressive and to reinforce the human/sustainability dimension.

A good homepage rhythm would be approximately:

**Dark hero → dark problem → slightly lighter technical section → light mission/science section → dark infrastructure section → dark research section → light open-science/institution section → dark footer**

The transitions should feel architectural rather than decorative.

---

# 6. Color Usage Rule

Approximately:

* 70–80% graphite / dark neutral
* 15–20% white / pale mineral
* 5–10% teal-green and other accents

Green should function like punctuation.

**Never flood the website in green.**

The sustainability message should primarily come from the **mission and conceptual imagery**, not the amount of green pigment on screen.

---

# 7. Typography

Typography is one of the strongest opportunities to differentiate AFH from academic scientific software.

The typography should resemble a top-tier infrastructure company.

## Primary typeface

Preferred:

**Geist**

Use throughout the interface and most body copy.

Fallback:

`Inter, ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif`

Geist has the technical clarity and polish appropriate for the brand without becoming visually eccentric.

---

## Monospace

Preferred:

**Geist Mono**

or:

**IBM Plex Mono**

Use sparingly for:

* project names
* architecture labels
* simulation metadata
* small technical annotations
* code-like diagrams
* scientific labels

Do not render large amounts of normal prose in monospace.

---

# 8. Typography Character

Headlines should be:

* large
* clean
* relatively tight
* declarative
* not excessively bold

Avoid the enormous ultra-bold startup typography where four words consume an entire viewport.

Preferred headline weights:

`500–600`

Body:

`400–450`

Labels:

`500`

Technical labels can use slightly increased letter spacing.

---

# 9. Typography Scale

Suggested desktop ranges:

Hero headline:
`clamp(3.5rem, 7vw, 6.5rem)`

Major section heading:
`clamp(2.4rem, 4vw, 4.2rem)`

Secondary heading:
`1.5–2.2rem`

Body:
`1.05–1.2rem`

Small metadata:
`0.8–0.9rem`

Maximum prose width:

approximately `650–760px`.

Avoid very wide lines.

---

# 10. Logo Concept

## Primary concept

The logo should combine:

**atomic structure + Earth**

without becoming literal clip art.

Conceptually:

* a minimal spherical nucleus
* the nucleus contains a highly simplified suggestion of Earth
* three orbital paths surround the nucleus
* six electron points are distributed across the orbital system
* orbital paths should be geometric and thin
* electron points should be small and precise

The identity should read immediately as **atomic**, while the Earth component should reveal itself on closer inspection.

---

# 11. Logo Simplification

The biggest risk is trying to place a detailed globe inside a tiny nucleus.

Do **not** draw continents realistically.

Instead, the nucleus should be a circle containing perhaps:

* one curved meridian
* one or two abstract land/water divisions

or even simply:

* a circular teal sphere
* one subtle orbital/latitude line

At small sizes, it should remain recognizable as a spherical world-like object without requiring geographic detail.

The logo should work at approximately `24px`.

---

# 12. Logo Geometry

Preferred style:

* thin geometric strokes
* no gradients required
* consistent stroke thickness
* symmetrical or near-symmetrical orbital structure
* six small electron dots
* circular central nucleus

Avoid:

* cartoon atom symbols
* glowing neon electrons
* thick swooshes
* realistic Earth imagery
* gradients mimicking 3D globes
* excessive orbital complexity

---

# 13. Logo Colors

Primary dark-background version:

* orbital paths: muted off-white / gray
* electrons: teal-green
* nucleus/Earth: teal-green with minimal pale detail

Light-background version:

* orbital paths: graphite
* electrons: dark teal
* nucleus: teal-green

The logo should also function monochromatically.

---

# 14. Wordmark

The wordmark should simply be:

**Atoms for Humanity**

Set in the primary sans-serif.

Do not invent an ornate custom logotype for the initial launch.

Use approximately:

* medium weight
* normal capitalization
* tight but natural letter spacing

Avoid all-uppercase branding.

---

# 15. Temporary Logo Strategy

The website should **not be delayed by logo development**.

If the logo cannot be implemented elegantly for launch, use:

**Atoms for Humanity**

as a high-quality typographic wordmark.

A poor atom/Earth illustration would damage the brand more than having no symbol.

---

# 16. Layout Philosophy

The layout should be:

**spacious, modular, architectural, and highly aligned.**

Use a maximum content width around:

`1200–1320px`.

Pages should have generous horizontal margins.

Use a 12-column grid internally where useful.

Most sections should have significant vertical breathing room:

approximately:

`96–160px`

desktop section padding.

Mobile:

`64–96px`.

---

# 17. Visual Density

The website should avoid both extremes:

### Not dense like scientific documentation

and

### not dramatically sparse like a luxury fashion brand.

There should usually be one major conceptual idea per screen.

Visitors should feel that the organization has **depth without clutter**.

---

# 18. Navigation

Navigation should be extremely simple.

Left:

**AFH mark + Atoms for Humanity**

Right:

* Our Work
* Technology
* About
* Team
* Organization

Optional:

* GitHub icon/link

No enormous CTA button is necessary.

A subtle **Contact** link or outlined button is acceptable.

---

# 19. Navigation Treatment

The navigation should initially sit naturally within the hero.

When scrolling:

* become sticky
* use a slightly translucent graphite background
* subtle backdrop blur
* thin low-contrast bottom border

Avoid dramatic glassmorphism.

The effect should barely be noticed.

---

# 20. Hero Design

The homepage hero is the most important visual statement.

It should feel:

**large, calm, technical, ambitious.**

Suggested structure:

Small eyebrow:

`ATOMS FOR HUMANITY`

Large headline:

> **Building a sustainable molecular future.**

Supporting copy:

> Modern civilization is built from molecules. We are developing the scientific capabilities needed to discover and design chemistry for human and environmental health.

Then perhaps:

`Explore our work →`

---

# 21. Hero Visual

Avoid hero photography.

Instead create an **extremely restrained scientific visual system**.

Preferred direction:

A large, abstract molecular / atomic computational field occupying part of the hero.

Possible visual:

* sparse particles or atomic nodes
* thin orbital paths
* a few molecular connections
* subtle spatial grid
* faint simulated trajectory lines
* restrained teal illumination

The visual should suggest:

**matter being computationally explored**

rather than depict a literal molecule.

It should occupy perhaps the right 40–50% of the hero on large screens and recede or simplify dramatically on mobile.

---

# 22. Hero Animation

Optional and subtle.

Examples:

* very slow orbital drift
* a few nodes gradually changing position
* faint connection lines appearing/disappearing
* extremely slow parallax
* soft teal pulses representing computation

Motion should run on timescales of several seconds.

Never:

* spinning molecule rapidly
* particle explosion
* mouse-chasing effects
* interactive starfield
* animated Matrix code
* constant glowing gradients

The visitor should barely consciously notice the movement.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

---

# 23. Scientific Visual Language

AFH should develop a consistent visual vocabulary derived from real scientific concepts.

Possible motifs:

### Molecular graphs

Nodes connected by precise lines.

### Reaction pathways

Branching networks.

### Energy landscapes

Smooth curves or contour lines.

### Simulation trajectories

Sequences of points or paths.

### Orbital geometry

Ellipses and spatial paths.

### Computational grids

Fine geometric systems.

### Data pipelines

Simple connected systems showing infrastructure layers.

These should be abstracted and simplified.

---

# 24. Scientific Accuracy

Scientific visuals do not always need to represent an actual experiment, but they should never look scientifically nonsensical.

Avoid decorative pseudo-science such as:

* impossible molecules
* random DNA helices
* arbitrary equations
* atom symbols with incorrect labels
* fake terminal outputs
* generic chemistry equations used as texture

A scientifically sophisticated audience should not find the design embarrassing.

---

# 25. Technical Architecture Diagrams

This is an area where AFH can strongly differentiate itself.

Architecture diagrams should be visually excellent.

Use:

* simple rectangular or rounded modules
* thin connector lines
* restrained accent color
* excellent typography
* ample space
* clear conceptual flow

Example:

**Researchers / AI Agents**

↓

**Scientific Workflows**

↓

**ChemCloud**

↓

**BigChem**

↓

**qc***

↓

**Simulation Engines**

↓

**CPU / GPU / Cloud / HPC**

This diagram should look closer to excellent infrastructure documentation than a scientific PowerPoint slide.

---

# 26. The AI Visual Language

Do not use:

* brains
* robots
* glowing humanoids
* AI chip imagery
* neural-network stock graphics

Instead show AI as another participant in the scientific system.

A diagram might show:

**Researcher**
and
**AI Agent**

both connecting into:

**AFH Digital Laboratory**

That communicates the idea far more credibly.

---

# 27. Sustainability Visual Language

Sustainability should be suggested through:

* teal-green color
* organic curvature mixed with technical geometry
* language about human and environmental health
* occasional biological or material motifs
* light mineral sections providing warmth

Do not use:

* leaves
* forests
* hands holding plants
* globes covered in leaves
* solar-panel stock imagery
* generic green gradients

This is **molecular sustainability**, not conventional environmental branding.

---

# 28. Section Labels

Small section labels can be useful.

Example:

`01 — THE CHALLENGE`

`02 — THE OPPORTUNITY`

`03 — THE INFRASTRUCTURE`

Use either:

* small sans serif
* or subtle monospace

with teal accent.

This helps create a precise engineered feeling.

Do not number every component obsessively.

---

# 29. Borders and Surfaces

Use subtle borders rather than shadows.

Example:

`1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.08)`

Cards should normally differ from backgrounds only slightly.

Avoid floating card overload.

Not every paragraph needs to be placed inside a rounded rectangle.

---

# 30. Border Radius

Preferred:

`8–14px`

Not:

`24–40px`

The site should feel engineered rather than bubbly.

Buttons can use approximately:

`8px`.

---

# 31. Buttons

Buttons should be simple.

Primary:

teal-green background with very dark text.

Secondary:

transparent / graphite background with subtle border and light text.

Avoid:

* pill buttons everywhere
* gradient buttons
* animated glowing outlines

Example:

`Explore our work →`

not:

`🚀 START DISCOVERING THE FUTURE`

---

# 32. Links

Text links should often use:

* off-white text
* subtle underline or arrow
* teal hover state

Prefer restrained directional indicators:

`→`

---

# 33. Cards

Cards should only be used when the content genuinely represents discrete things:

* software projects
* research programs
* people
* governance documents

Cards should not become the dominant page layout.

The homepage should primarily feel like **continuous editorial storytelling**, not a dashboard.

---

# 34. Software Project Cards

qc*, BigChem, and ChemCloud can use clean technical cards.

Each card can contain:

**Project name**

One-sentence description

Small category label:

`OPEN SOURCE`

Optional:

`GitHub →`

Possible tiny architecture or code motif.

Do not add fake product screenshots merely to fill space.

---

# 35. Research Presentation

Research should feel like **real science**, not product marketing.

Use:

* simple research themes
* concise descriptions
* occasionally one authentic figure or molecular graphic
* references/publications when available

Avoid massive promotional claims.

A section title like:

> **Building the tools—and using them to do science.**

is ideal.

---

# 36. Light Sections

Use one or two light sections to dramatically change the emotional register.

Best candidates:

### Mission / sustainable molecular future

or

### Open science / public good

Background:

`#EDF3F0`

Text:

dark graphite.

Accent:

dark teal.

The transition from dark technical infrastructure into a light open-science section can visually express:

> **technology in service of humanity**

without saying so visually in an obvious way.

---

# 37. Team Page

The Team page should remain highly restrained.

No people on the homepage.

Team page layout:

large page heading

brief institutional introduction

then profile cards.

Headshots should:

* share identical aspect ratios
* use neutral backgrounds where possible
* be professionally lit but not excessively corporate
* avoid dramatically different cropping

Preferred headshot treatment:

approximately square or `4:5`.

Cards:

* image
* name
* title
* short biography
* optional links

Do not use enormous employee portraits.

---

# 38. Governance Page

The governance page should prioritize trust and readability over design spectacle.

This can be one of the lightest pages on the site.

Suggested structure:

large title

brief transparency statement

organization facts in a well-designed two-column grid

then:

**Corporate & Tax Documents**

with document rows/cards.

Each document:

* simple document icon
* title
* one-line description
* `View PDF →`

This page should feel almost like beautifully designed institutional documentation.

---

# 39. Document Cards

Example:

**Certificate of Incorporation**
Official formation document for Atoms for Humanity.
`View PDF →`

No preview thumbnails needed.

---

# 40. Footer

Dark footer.

It should contain:

**Atoms for Humanity**

short mission sentence

navigation

contact information

physical address

nonprofit status

GitHub

copyright

Optional small brand statement:

> Building scientific infrastructure for a sustainable molecular future.

The footer is an important verification surface and should not be minimal to the point of hiding organizational identity.

---

# 41. Imagery

The first version of the website does **not** need large quantities of images.

Preferred visual hierarchy:

1. typography
2. layout
3. scientific diagrams
4. generated/custom scientific graphics
5. authentic scientific figures where useful
6. photographs only on Team

This will make the site much more distinctive than filling it with stock imagery.

---

# 42. Background Texture

Dark surfaces can contain an almost imperceptible technical texture.

Possibilities:

* extremely faint coordinate grid
* fine noise
* sparse dots
* subtle topology contours

Opacity should generally be below approximately `3–6%`.

Visitors should feel depth without immediately seeing “a background pattern.”

---

# 43. Gradients

Use sparingly.

Good:

a very subtle radial teal illumination behind a scientific visual.

Example conceptual treatment:

deep graphite → slightly lighter teal graphite.

Bad:

large green-blue-purple startup gradients.

The brand should remain predominantly **flat and material**.

---

# 44. Motion Philosophy

Motion communicates technical quality when subtle.

Use:

* smooth 150–250ms interface transitions
* slight card elevation or border changes
* understated link movement
* very slow scientific hero animation

Avoid:

* scroll hijacking
* elaborate page transitions
* excessive fade-ins
* elements flying in from every direction
* GSAP-heavy spectacle
* loading animations

The website should remain fast.

---

# 45. Scroll Animations

If section reveals are used:

* opacity from ~0.85 to 1
* translate upward by perhaps `8–16px`
* duration around `400–600ms`

Only once.

Do not animate every paragraph separately.

---

# 46. Performance

The sophistication of the site should come from **design quality, not application complexity**.

The production website should be:

* static
* extremely fast
* minimal JavaScript
* responsive
* accessible
* indexable by search engines
* functional without client-side application logic

Avoid unnecessary frameworks or animation packages if CSS can achieve the same effect.

---

# 47. Mobile Behavior

The mobile version must feel intentionally designed.

On small screens:

* reduce visual complexity
* remove or simplify background graphics
* stack architecture diagrams cleanly
* preserve generous whitespace
* keep body text at least approximately 16px
* convert navigation into a simple menu
* ensure governance information remains immediately readable

Do not simply shrink the desktop site.

---

# 48. Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA.

Requirements:

* strong color contrast
* visible focus indicators
* semantic HTML
* proper heading hierarchy
* descriptive links
* alt text for meaningful images
* decorative SVGs marked appropriately
* keyboard navigation
* reduced-motion support

The teal accent should never be relied upon alone to convey meaning.

---

# 49. Tone of Copy Within the Design

Copy should be:

* short
* declarative
* technically literate
* calm
* ambitious

Good:

> **A computational laboratory for molecular discovery.**

> Molecular simulation can become a scientific capability operating at massive scale.

> Build the infrastructure. Use it to discover better chemistry.

Bad:

> Revolutionizing the future of AI-powered molecular discovery.

> The world's most advanced chemistry innovation platform.

> Transforming everything through next-generation AI.

---

# 50. Visual Differentiation From Academic Quantum Chemistry

This distinction is strategically important.

Typical scientific software websites often exhibit:

* dense navigation
* default documentation themes
* inconsistent typography
* low-quality diagrams
* GitHub-centric identity
* clutter
* technical terminology before mission
* little thought about visual hierarchy

AFH should deliberately do the opposite.

The website should demonstrate the same qualities AFH wants its software to embody:

**coherence**

**interoperability**

**simplicity**

**precision**

**engineering discipline**

**care**

The website itself should almost function as evidence that AFH understands how to build excellent infrastructure.

---

# 51. Visual Differentiation From AI Startups

At the same time, avoid:

* black + purple gradients
* chrome or metallic typography
* glowing neural networks
* impossible 3D worlds
* giant “AI” messaging
* excessive moving particles
* exaggerated futurism

AFH should feel like it will still look credible in ten years.

---

# 52. Design Reference Mix

Conceptually weight the references approximately:

**45% Linear / Vercel / Stripe-style software sophistication**

**30% modern scientific research institution**

**15% scientific publication / technical documentation**

**10% sustainability / biological warmth**

The environmental component should change the *character* of the technical aesthetic, not replace it.

---

# 53. Homepage Emotional Progression

The homepage should intentionally move through four emotional states.

### 1. Ambition

**Building a sustainable molecular future.**

Large dark hero.

### 2. Intellectual recognition

**The problem is fundamentally about our ability to discover better chemistry.**

Minimal explanatory storytelling.

### 3. Technological excitement

**Simulation + compute + AI → a scalable digital laboratory.**

This is the most visually technical portion.

### 4. Purpose and trust

**Open science, research, nonprofit stewardship, people, organization.**

Introduce lightness and institutional credibility.

This gives the page an emotional narrative rather than merely a sequence of sections.

# 59. Final Design Test

Before the design is accepted, evaluate it against these questions.

### Does it look like excellent software engineers built it?

If no, redesign.

### Could it be mistaken for a university research group's website?

If yes, redesign.

### Could it be mistaken for a generic AI startup?

If yes, reduce futurism.

### Could it be mistaken for an environmental advocacy nonprofit?

If yes, reduce conventional sustainability imagery.

### Does sustainability remain perceptible?

If no, strengthen the mission language, teal signal, and moments of visual warmth.

### Does the site feel scientifically credible?

If no, simplify visual metaphors and improve technical diagrams.

### Does the site feel calm and trustworthy?

If no, reduce visual activity.

### Does it still feel ambitious?

If no, increase typography scale and conceptual clarity rather than adding decoration.

---

# 60. Desired Final Impression

The ideal first impression is:

> **Atoms for Humanity feels like a new kind of scientific institution: mission-driven like a nonprofit research organization, technically rigorous like an excellent software infrastructure company, and ambitious about using computation to change what humanity can discover about matter.**

The dark technical environment communicates computational sophistication.

The teal-green signal connects that capability to living systems and sustainability.

The restrained scientific imagery communicates seriousness.

The exceptional typography, spacing, diagrams, and interaction design communicate engineering discipline.

The mission provides the reason all of that technology exists.

That combination—not any individual graphic—is the Atoms for Humanity brand.
